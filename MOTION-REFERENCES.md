# Character and skating motion

The motion design follows inline-skating instruction from Asha Kirkby of Skatefresh:

- [Push, support and recovery](https://skatefresh.com/how-to-get-a-more-powerful-skating-stride/)
- [Intermediate speed-skating posture](https://skatefresh.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Speed-Eng-Intermediate-Focus-Points.pdf)

Version 14 rebuilds her head and hair from her real photographs: a head measured from her frontal portrait, with one fixed expression, and braided hair from her skating photos. The outfit from the eight-view reference sheet, the skeleton, bone lengths and skating motion are retained. Every view shares the same volumetric geometry and joints; camera changes never alter the pose or flatten the upper body. Her face does not change while she skates, balances or jumps.

`src/skating-motion.js` drives support and glide → sideways/backward push → low recovery → landing and weight transfer. Positions use quintic curves with zero endpoint velocity, alternating left and right. The recovering foot rises approximately 5.8 cm, with quintic easing to keep velocity continuous. Stride length changes smoothly with speed; relaxed arms counter-swing with weight transfer. Stopping the push smoothly settles into gliding. Jump tuck follows jump height, while arm tuck uses a separate smooth transition. Landing compresses the pose before recovery. A one-foot trick waits for the support skate to land, gradually lifts the other foot, and blends back out. Hair follows the body continuously: the twin braids sway on a damped spring and slide over the shirt instead of passing through it.

`src/skater-legs.js` solves hip, knee and ankle positions with two-bone 3D inverse kinematics. Thigh and shin lengths remain fixed at 0.35 and 0.34 game-world units, with forward-bending knees. Each mesh segment rotates rigidly, and the skates stay attached to the ankles. Four spaced wheels rotate with speed. Side and rear views render the same 3D legs rather than changing directional images. Turns adjust the hips and upper body while grounded wheels maintain a horizontal contact plane.

`src/zhiguo-identity.js` calibrates the current portrait's facial proportions and texture mapping on a continuous 3D head and neck. The selected earlier portrait supplies the eyes, glasses and smile. Forehead-to-jaw seams are welded to prevent a visible lighting split. Earlier version 8 projected the original photograph using hairline, eye, nose, smile and chin landmarks; that source photo remains available for comparison. The continuous head surface avoids generic eye-socket and mouth openings obscuring the photographed features. Ears, glasses arms and earrings are three-dimensional. The front glasses come from the portrait, avoiding duplicate frames.

`src/skater-body.js` retains anatomical arm and hand meshes. The buttoned blue-and-lavender plaid shirt has a pointed collar, a curved shirt-tail hem and a green flower pendant on a fine chain. Sleeves cover the shoulder skin, and the hem clears the denim shorts during movement. Skate cuffs pivot at the ankle with the shin, like real hinged cuffs. Two-bone IK drives continuous arm skin, blending the elbow between segments. Each finger has a relaxed joint-based pose. Version 12 mirrors the hand binding coordinates and reverses triangle winding so the thumbs have the correct anatomical direction. The upper body and both hip joints share pelvis position and small rotations. The spine leans forward and shifts toward the support foot; shoulders and arms counter-swing with each step. `src/zhiguo.js` coordinates body and leg updates and attaches head and mouth anchors to the actual joints.

`src/mesh-batching.js` merges only fixed attachments within the same joint. Moving arms, legs and rolling wheels remain separate. Reduced grain and high-speed chromatic effects, plus nighttime fill lighting, help preserve the character outline and plaid detail. Frame rate depends on the device.

## Validation

`npm test` checks head and torso volume, pelvis attachment, arm lengths and swing continuity, connected skin and fingers, her head's proportions and smooth surface, that the face geometry and texture stay unchanged through skating, balancing and jumping, braid movement, and anatomical hand direction. It also checks leg lengths at four speeds, forward knee bends, skate-to-ankle attachment, wheel penetration and support contact, cadence when gliding, turn transitions and one-foot tricks. Twelve-second sequences at 30/60/120 Hz cover real jump heights, landing, stopping and restarting, turns and balance, checking world-space joint continuity and consistency across refresh rates.

Browser checks cover eight horizontal views, face/upper-body/top framing, desktop and mobile layouts, jump and landing, motion switches, balance tricks and opening the character HTML without a server. With animation frozen, camera rotation must leave the head's world matrix unchanged, preventing a camera-facing photo plane from replacing the 3D head.
